My research program sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computational biology, and clinical hepatology. I develop machine learning methods that use molecular and clinical data to improve diagnosis, risk stratification, and treatment decision-making in chronic liver disease and liver transplantation.

## Research vision

The long-term goal of my work is to build clinically useful, interpretable, and externally validated AI systems that can help clinicians understand complex liver-disease biology and personalize patient care. I am especially interested in models that can integrate high-dimensional omics with routinely available clinical variables and translate the output into actionable clinical summaries.

## Core areas

### 1. cfDNA methylation biomarkers for liver transplantation

I work on cell-free DNA methylation approaches for non-invasive assessment of liver graft injury. This includes methylation-based tissue-of-origin inference, discovery of disease-associated methylation signatures, and multimodal models that integrate methylation features with clinical variables to distinguish immune-mediated and metabolic graft injury.

### 2. Multimodal machine learning for liver disease

I develop models that combine clinical data with methylation, transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and imaging-derived data. These models are designed to address clinically important questions such as post-transplant MASH recurrence, graft rejection, liver regeneration, and hepatocellular carcinoma recurrence.

### 3. Interpretable AI for clinical translation

Because clinical adoption requires trust, my work emphasizes interpretable and transparent models. I use approaches such as SHAP, attention-based attribution, feature-selection stability, and biologically grounded pathway-level modeling to connect model predictions to plausible disease mechanisms.

### 4. Foundation models and representation learning for omics

I am interested in self-supervised, contrastive, and foundation-model approaches for high-dimensional molecular datasets. These methods can learn reusable biological representations from large omics datasets and then be fine-tuned for specific liver-disease prediction tasks.
